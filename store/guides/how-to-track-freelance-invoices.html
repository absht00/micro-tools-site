How to Track Freelance Invoices Without an Accounting Suite

A spreadsheet, four columns and one rule: every invoice is written down the day it is sent. That is the whole system.

Most freelancers lose money not because clients refuse to pay, but because nobody noticed the invoice went past its due date.

The minimum viable setup is a single sheet with one row per invoice: number, client, issue date, due date, amount, payment status. Six columns. Nothing else is required to know who owes you what today.

The second column that pays for itself is days overdue. Once an invoice passes the due date, the number becomes positive and it is obvious which client needs the reminder email today rather than next month.

The third is the tax set-aside. Decide a percentage once, apply it to every payment received, and put that money aside in a separate account. This is the part that turns a good month into a survivable year.
