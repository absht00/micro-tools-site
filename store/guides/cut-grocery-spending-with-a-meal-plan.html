How a 30-Day Meal Plan Cuts the Grocery Bill

Groceries are the most controllable line in a household budget, and the least controlled. The lever is not buying cheaper food, it is buying known food.

Impulse spending at the supermarket happens when the list is vague. A plan that already says what you eat for the next thirty days produces a shopping list with quantities, and a list with quantities leaves far less room for improvisation.

The second saving is repetition of ingredients. A plan built around a handful of base ingredients buys those ingredients in larger packs, which lowers the unit price and reduces waste because nothing stays in the fridge unopened.

The third is the one people underestimate: time. Deciding what to cook every evening at 19:30 is how takeaway happens. A plan removes that decision, and the takeaway usually costs more than the planned meal it replaces.

Write your own supermarket prices next to the quantities once. After that the monthly total is accurate for where you actually shop, and it updates itself when prices change.
